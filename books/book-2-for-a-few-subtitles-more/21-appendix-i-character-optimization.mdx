# Appendix I: Character Optimization

## Character Optimization

Character optimization is considered a dirty word in some places. This is because it
allows some characters to be better at stuff than other characters. Now, normally this isn't an
issue. We don't care that in combat the barbarian is awesome, the assassin is a glass cannon, and
the cleric is just sort of tagging along and helping out. That's because we assume that the assassin is
better at stealthy stuff than the other two, the cleric is good at knowing stuff and casting spells
where the others can't, and that all of this is relevant in a game. Where the dislike for 'char-op'
comes from is when a game is only about combat or the cleric manages to be better at combat than
the barbarian, better at stealthy stuff than the assassin, and is still the only person who knows
stuff and casts spells. Of course that may not be due to any sort of character building, it could be
inherent in the game.
In DUNGEONS THE DRAGONING everyone is (supposed to be) just so awesome that
we don't need to care about fiddly pop "balance" or anything. Some effort has gone into trying to
make every exaltation pretty good in their own way (that's why Werewolves got a
re-write, they had issues). We've fiddled with the classes a little to make your choice of class track
matter a little less. You're still going to want to stick to the fighty type classes for fighting and the
magical classes for spell casting, but there's been an attempt to make them all a bit more even and
get them all to have more than just 'fight' and
'cast spell' as options. We'll explore some of the extremes in our sample characters.
Note & Assumptions: With the rewrite you can take up to four hindrances, upping
starting character XP to 1000. We're assuming that the player gets to apply that XP in any order
and that everything taken applies before they spend the next chunk of XP. We also assume that
when selecting the attribute groups that you can choose from all six groups, vertical and horizontal.
We'll do everything by the character creation steps; 1) Attributes, 2)
Skills, 3) Species, 4) Exaltation, 5) Class, 6)
Backgrounds, 7) Alignment, 8) XP, 9) the
Equipment package (actually we ignore this one most of the time). We also have a very strong
tendency to take the Veteran O' the Wheel asset. It costs 100 XP and a semihindrance, but if it cranks an attribute up to 5

dots that's 400 XP you aren't paying later. Add getting a skill up to 5 and that's another 200 XP
of value. All the class tracks require two skills at 5 to get into the 5th level, so if you're playing the
long game it's completely worth it. If you're playing a short game you should consider it
anyways since the three combat skills use the skill level as kept dice, and kept dice are a good thing.

**Mega-Evoker.** This is how you start out throwing around enough magic to sometimes
one-shot a lesser daemon.
Attributes: Charisma 4Skills: Academic Lore 3, Forbidden Lore 3, and
social skills to leverage that Charisma
Species: Any, perhaps Gnome for weapon & armor proficiency or Assimar for their racial asset to add Charisma dice to a damage roll
Exaltation: Atlantean, magic: Evocation (Magic Missile), reroll warp effects
Class: Initiate
Backgrounds: optional (Artifact 3)
Alignment: likely Tezeench
XP:

- 400 XP, Divine Ministration, Hatred (Heretics), Peer (Your Religion), Minor Magic (Healing or Abjuration)
- The character has completed their 1st level class and advances to the Priest class
- 200 XP, Atlantean Power stat +1, can spend 1 Resource Point to get +3 level & magic school for a Focus Power test
- 1 Hindrance: 100 XP, Veteran O' The Wheel, +1 Charisma (5), +1 to any skill

Optional: 1 hindrance, +2 Artifact, Wraithbone bionic heart (reroll warp effects again)
This character can spend a Resource Point to roll
9k5(~41) on the Focus Power test of Magic
Missile while counting as a level 5 character for it. That averages five raises on the spell which
gets us six missiles doing an average of 10 damage each. The default lesser incarnate daemon has 4
Resilience and 9 HP, meaning that it takes 2 wounds per missile and dropping it (on average)
to a crit 1 & crit 2. Energy crit 2 to the body is
1d5 levels of Fatigue on it's 3 Constitution, that gives us a (40% for body location times 40% for
4+ on 1d5) 16% chance of a Fatigue KO on a lesser incarnate daemon for a single Half Action
and a Resource Point. The casting is quite safe as you would have to roll five 10s in order to be
forced to roll for Warp effects. Plus the character rolls twice (four time if the optional hindrances
were taken) for any warp effects and chooses which one they like. Nothing is stopping this
character from loading up on physical attributes,
Acrobatics, Ballistics, and buying a nice gun. Or they could have 3s in the mental attributes and
load up on brainy skills.

**Everything.** This is a character that does everything, and not badly either.
Attributes: All attributes will end up at 3 dots.
Skills: All skills will end up at 1 dot.
Species: Human, +1 Hero Point
Exaltation: Paragon, +1 Hero Point, free racial feat: Mixed Heritage [Tiefling]
Class: choose from Rat Catcher, Mercenary,
Scholar, Initiate, Peasant.
Backgrounds: Inheritance 3, best quality cortex implant
Alignment: whatever
XP: 100 XP Tiefling racial asset: Beneficial
Mutation

- 100 XP, brings a low attribute to 2
- 100 XP Veteran O' The Wheel
- 300 XP, one dot in 3 class skills
- 3 hindrances: one dot each in 3 more
  class skills

Optional: another hindrance for the Academy asset

Roll 4k3 for everything except the fighting skills, and you have a bonus on some
lore tests. Sure, you're no great shakes in combat yet (aiming and/or ganging up helps). But if you
start in the Mercenary class you can spend 400 XP for your basic weapon
proficiency and raise weaponry (or brawling) and ballistics both to 3, putting you at 4k3.
Then another 100 XP gets you light or medium armor. In the meantime you can drop prone,
Full Action aim, and then full-auto burst for 4k2 with an autopistol or lasgun. In theory you can
very nearly duplicate this character with different species and classes (one or two attributes will be
at 2), but being Human makes it easy. If you don't mind having zero dots in some skills you
can lose the hindrances or start in any Level 1 class by moving some skills around. Is this
character amazing? No, not yet. But they can do anything; thievery, magic, fighting, engineering,
schmoozing with the rich and famous, captaining a starship. Oh, and there's the 4 Hero Points for
rerolls plus 3 pressure points so you can't fail a roll by 1 point. Get to 3rd level and buy your
Power stat up to 3 (OK, that's like 1300 or so
XP away, but it's a goal) and you'll have 9 pressure points while you get 3 back every
round. Missing a TN by one or two points is something you don't have to worry about. If you
don't like hindrances you can have three skills that you aren't good at.

Brawling instead of Weaponry, using a Power

Fist and Setting Sun (you may want to change the class to Brother then). You could also move
into the realm of Artifact weapons. And this character has no reason not to put some points
into the social or mental attributes and skills, just to round out their non-combat capabilities.
Final AdviceLow attributes are weak points for your character and high attributes are great because
they're both rolled and kept dice. Naturally things are set up so they you have to have low attributes
to have high ones. In the original edition almost all magical saves were Arcana + Willpower, with
a few Arcana + Constitution or Arcana + Wisdom saves. This revision has worked to vary the saves
(and fear effects) so that Willpower isn't a sort of super-attribute. To this end a number of spells
now call for saving throws using Size, Intelligence, Fellowship, Composure, Strength, and
other attributes. Fear and insanity were changed too to use Composure and Wisdom in addition
to Willpower. While you're perfectly free to make a combat whore with 1s in Intelligence, Charisma,
Fellowship, and Composure, it's going to hurt you. It won't just be the rest of the party carrying
you through social encounters or puzzles either. A
Mind Flayer can drop a 8k5 Stun spell on you averaging over 35 on the Focus Power test,
meaning you need a 26 or higher Arcana +
Composure save to not be Stunned for a round and then lose two Half Actions after that. You
need around a 6k3 to average that, and being
Stunned isn't the worst thing that can happen.
