# advanced rules: Tracking

## Following Tracks

Look tracks. See tracks. Follow tracks.
Simple, eh? Oh, you want actual advice or something? OK, here goes.
The default TN to track something is 15. After that you can make a guess or use the
following modifiers as guidelines. This does not cover you trying to follow someone without being
seen, or asking people "Which way did he go?"
Numbers: Bigger stuff is easier to track and groups of things are also easier to track.

- Subtract the number of creatures after the
  first one from the TN. Thirty elephants are really easy to track.
- Subtract twice the size of the largest creature if
  they have to squeeze, wiggle, twist, or otherwise compress and adjust how they move just to get
  through the place. A horse walking through that office building is going to leave marks.
  Terrain: The type of terrain dictates how well it retains signs of passage and also involves
  how well the signs blend in. Tracking someone carrying a dripping can of dark paint is easy on a
  white linoleum floor, but hard across a newly tarred concrete parking lot.
- -10 for terrain that easily retains signs of
  passage, such as a sandy beach or a lightly snow covered field.
- +10 for terrain that just won't retain signs
  of passage, such as most building interiors and running water.

**Entity:** Some things leave more tracks and some things leave fewer tracks, of course you
don't need to check for stuff that just doesn't leave any tracks (but you can make the players roll
anyways).

- -20 for things that leave obvious marks like
  giant slugs with meter wide slime trails
- -10 for wounded things that seriously bleed or
  leak like heavily wounded people and vehicles leaking fuel
- +10 for things that don't leave decent marks of
  their passage, such as a small snake in the grass or a person walking through a room without
  touching stuff
- +20 for creatures with the Phasing quality if they leave at least some ectoplasm, like in Ghostbusters.
  Senses: Dogs can smell better than eagles but eagles have better eyesight, just don't try to smell
  a rabbit from 300m in the air or spot a black cat in the pitch dark night.
- -10 for tracking the creature with a sense it does
  not possess or does not know about. Like escapees who don't know they're being tracked with dogs.
- +10 if the creature knows what sense is
  primarily being used to track it and can do something about it, such as something able to
  change it's scent when it is being tracked by dogs.
- +20 for using the wrong sense for the job. It's
  no use trying to track by smell in a perfume shop or see where a cat walked through a house.
  Time: Different kinds of trails fade faster than others. Contrails fade fast but footprints in
  mud can be fossilized. Blood has a distinctive smell that can linger even after it dries but heavy
  snow can cover up footprints very quickly.
- -20 if the creature just moved out of sight or
  hearing range and left fresh spoor. This is for following fresh footprints in the mud or a wheeled
  vehicle that just recently raced across a sandy beach.
- -10 if nothing major has happened to obscure
  the signs like the floor hasn't been cleaned since the wounded person passed by.
- +10 when stuff has happened that covered or
  removed some of the signs of passage like, someone wiped up most of the blood on the floor.
- +20 if it's been long enough that there are very
  few signs left. This is for stuff a couple days old, or trying to follow a motorcycle track on a beach
  after the tide has come in once.
  Identification: Sometimes you don't know exactly what you're tracking but you can make a
  guess based on the tracks, or perhaps you need to know if there are hobbits mixed in among the ork
  raiding party.
- -20 for easily identifiable marks. It's easy to tell
  rabbit versus cougar tracks or very fresh blood versus hour old blood.
- -10 when distinguishing features and context
  gives you a decent guess. In an ork raiding band someone wearing a boot on one foot and the
  other foot looks like a robot it's probably an Ork
  Nob and likely to be the leader.
- +10 when there is not much information except
  maybe a clue or two is in there somewhere. This is for trying to figure out just how heavily weighed
  down with treasure were those pack horses, or if they were they carrying wounded soldiers instead.
- +20 when it is difficult to make out signs or
  there are no specific markings. The thing that stepped on the car was really big, with clawed feet
  and a tail. But was it an immature Godzilla or a large tarasque? These things matter.
