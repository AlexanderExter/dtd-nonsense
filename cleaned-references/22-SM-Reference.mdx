- **System assumptions:** Characters are action heroes; TN 10–20 covers most situations
- **Stunting:** 2–3 die stunts allow scene editing; failures narrated by the stunting player
- **Minions:** Use simplified Threat/Damage ratings for mook armies (see [Antagonists](./19-Antagonists.mdx))
- **Scale:** Personal → Vehicle → Ship; crossing scales requires SM ruling
- **XP:** Abstract Method (500/session) or Detailed Method (50–250 per encounter)
- **Off-list feats:** Cannot buy feats that aren't on your class lists, ever
- **Golden rule:** If it works the same for NPCs as PCs, it's probably fair
:::

## System Assumptions

### Character Competency

Characters are **action heroes**. Everyday activities don't require rolls.

- Mr. Normal (2 dots, no Drive skill) commutes for ten years without accidents
- Spider phobia requires a Goliath Bird-Eating Tarantula on your head or a horse-sized spider at 30 KPH to provoke TN 15 Fear(1)
- Common TNs are 10–20; over 20 = fewer than 1 in 10 can do it
- `8k4` is world-champion level — flying a fighter through a collapsing skyscraper = TN 35 (50% success for `8k4`)

**If multiple attempts are possible:** The question becomes how long it takes and whether they fail badly before succeeding.

### Player Input

Players have real input through:

- **Backgrounds** — chosen by players, signal interest in those setting aspects
- **Group feats/assets** (Peer, Hunted) — create shared narrative hooks
- **Stunting** — scene editing through creative narration

Only deny player input if it **completely conflicts** with established setting. Work out name/detail changes before refusing.

## Themes in Dungeons the Dragoning

The Great Wheel has key themes that distinguish it from other settings. Weaving these into your game helps it feel authentically set in the Great Wheel.

### The Ancient and the Forgotten

The Great Wheel's calendar runs from the disappearance of the Syrne over 40,000 years ago. There is an incredible depth of time—ages of galactic history destroyed in war or simply forgotten. The Great Wheel is filled with mysticism, fear, and superstition. Crystal Spheres lie unexplored, Syrneth ruins dot the stars, and the golden age of the galaxy is long past.

### Into the Unknown

Heroes are driven to become prolific explorers, charting pathways into Crystal Spheres and breaking ancient laws to open new Portal paths. Entire empires of aliens, lost worlds, and never-before-seen celestial phenomena can be found hidden within the folds of the known stars.

### Points of Light

Civilization is not the norm. Most of the galaxy is unexplored wilderness. Great empires and powerful worlds exist, but the void separates them by huge gulfs of time and space. Almost anything could happen to a world without anyone noticing for a long time.

### One Minute to Midnight

The galaxy teeters on the brink of ruin. Enemies lurk around every corner—daemons, modron automatons, vile aliens in the shadows. Across the Great Wheel, there is a constant struggle to survive. Doom fills the air, and the end is nigh.

## Game Styles

There is no one true way to play D:TD, but these style summaries provide inspiration and focus.

**"Vanilla" Dungeons the Dragoning:** Heroes return to the world, confront enemies, establish a power base, and become god-kings. Could emphasize rebuilding society, or focus on the long road to seizing Sigil itself. *Focus: No change.*

**By Your Powers Combined:** Over-the-top action cranked to eleven. Color-coded powered armor, drop pods into hot zones, fighting hundreds of enemies, melodramatic soap-opera relationships, orbital strikes. Think the golden age of the Syrne. *Focus: Artifact Background.*

**Outlander:** Exploration of alien worlds and new Crystal Spheres. Boldly go where no one has gone before. Highlight the differences between new expanses and familiar places. Don't make the Heroes the smallest fish—they're powerful on a large scale. *Focus: Lore skills.*

**The Clans:** All characters share a race, Exaltation, or faction, focusing on internal politics. Characters might be heroic special agents, a special team of experts, or members of the same clan or tribe. Shared background gives common ground. *Focus: Social Backgrounds, differing skillsets.*

### More Styles

- **Bolter and Chainsword with Daiklave:** Lighthearted and goofy. Teenagers at a Sigil academy, or proving their worth by finding the Lady of Pain a date. Played for laughs.
- **Invasion!:** A massive influx of otherworldly beings bent on conquering the Great Wheel. Fight, delay, or flee the unstoppable enemy. Maybe a Syrneth superweapon can stop them.
- **The Hero Who Came in from the Cold:** Intrigue and espionage for a Faction, Rogue Trader, or god. Trust no one. Brains, skills, and cunning over combat.
- **Sorcery & Sorcery:** Every character plays a sorcerer. Lean into sorcerous ambitions.
- **Walk the Earth:** A travelogue—characters wander the Wheel with no overriding purpose beyond cool standalone stories in new places.

## Forming a Party

### The Enemy of My Enemy

Characters are working against a common menace. They might be on the run from a powerful evil, or out to take down the same dark overlord. By the end of the first adventure they should decide to stick together for safety.

### The Gods Will It

Characters with similar alignments are thrust together by divine will. The Ruinous Powers, the Blessed Pantheon, or the Grey Council exert otherworldly impetus through prophetic dreams full of unfamiliar places. Works well with emphasis on mystical elements.

### Supernatural Conspiracy

Ambitious powers—Factions of Sigil, daemons, a cunning Pirate Prince—manipulate naive Heroes as tools. The characters begin serving a power supposedly greater than their own, but should have many opportunities to disabuse their controllers of this notion.

### Beyond the Call to Action

The characters knew one another before they became Exalted. Whatever their backgrounds, they stick together because of previous connections. So close a group all ascending together could easily become a major plot point.

## The Prelude

Run a short individual session with each player before the first full session. A prelude should be focused on roleplaying with few or no dice rolls. It provides a way to explore a character outside the normal pressures of the game and is useful for deciding what characters do during downtime. Preludes also serve as something to do when not everyone can show up for a full session.

## The First Session

Start with a bang. Instead of meeting in a dive bar before searching ruins, have them start with the artifact in hand, escaping from collapsing ruins while chased by beastmen hordes—then end with a relaxing tavern visit. Think James Bond cold open: action-packed culmination of an exciting adventure that ties into the plot later.

### First Session Guidelines

- **Go lenient with stunts:** Give examples of what characters can do. Encourage testing the system.
- **Don't kill characters:** Show the players how cool they can be, not how easily you can destroy them.
- **For timid players:** If characters are too cautious, tell them flat out that "dying" this session means incapacitation with a cool scar—no Hero Points burned. Only do this if players are being too timid. Knowing you're invincible doesn't make you brave.

### Reworking Characters

Allow players who were unsatisfied with their character's performance to rework after session 1. Take an active role with the player to smooth out rough spots. If a player wants to remove all non-combat skills after an action-heavy first session, encourage them to keep skills you foresee needing. Combat skills tip the balance in a fight, but non-combat skills can sway whole nations.

## Stunting

Two and three die stunts allow **scene editing** (cannot contradict existing content). If there needs to be a chandelier or trash chute, it exists unless it contradicts the scene.

- **Stunt rolls:** Ask for second roll only if really needed. TN never over 25, usually 10–20. Stunt dice apply to all stunt rolls.
- **Failure narration:** The stunting player narrates their own failure. No involuntary prat-falls.

## Scenery

**Blow it up.** Reinforced concrete = AP 16 cover. A strong guy with a power fist has 60% chance of 6+ damage past it. Running firefights through collapsing buildings = good action scenes.

### "No Empty Rooms" Rule

An empty hangar is a bad set. Add:

- Scaffolding, toolboxes, tire stacks
- Oil slicks, half an airplane, exploding barrels
- Things to throw enemies into (wood chippers, braziers, control panels)

Kicking over a brazier isn't a stunt. Picking up two braziers and banging them on someone's head like burning cymbals = stunt.

## Rulings & Improvisation

**Not everything is in the combat actions list.** SMs wing it. If conflicts arise, check: does it work the same for NPCs as PCs?

### Example: Throwing a Person

1. **Grapple** → get to hurt phase
2. **Strength Test** (TN = Size × 5, +1 per armor Resilience boost) to lift. Victim can escape next turn.
3. **Throw** next turn — another Test for distance = Strength meters + 1m per Raise
4. **Accurate throw** = Ballistics Test; damage = thrown person's Size as rolled dice, half as kept
5. Strength 7 werewolf is good at chucking halflings

### Character Sheets Don't Limit Actions

Players can: swing on chandeliers, bribe guards, light fires with Energy Ray, spike vampires to ceilings, punch cars to a stop, ride rockets into space by chaining themselves outside.

## Collaborative Storytelling

### SM Input

SMs can (and should) speak up:

- "I have an idea for this NPC"
- "I'd love today's session to explore this dungeon"
- Ask players questions: "Who did your character hate most in her home town?"

### Just In Case (Optional Rule)

Once per session, anyone can roll Level + Power stat (TN based on Level: 2→10, 10→20, 20→30, 30→40). No failure penalty. Caller asks one question + one per two Raises:

- "What can I use to ___?"
- "What is the biggest threat?"
- "What is the greatest danger?"
- "Who here is most vulnerable and how?"
- "How can we best end this quickly?"

## Combat Flow Guidance

Each game event is discrete. There are no truly simultaneous actions. When timing matters, use Initiative order; SM adjudicates edge cases.

### Common Timing Questions

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Two characters attack simultaneously? | Higher Initiative acts first. Second acts after resolution (even if unable). |
| Can I interrupt someone's action? | Only with specific Reactions (Parry, Dodge, Refute). No arbitrary interrupts. |
| Multiple effects trigger at once? | Active player resolves first. Same-turn effects: controlling player chooses order. |

## Scale Interactions

Personal, Vehicle, and Ship scales **do not interact directly**. Crossing scales requires narrative justification and SM ruling.

### Personal vs. Vehicle

- Characters can board, sabotage, or attack from inside
- Personal weapons generally cannot harm vehicles except called shots to vulnerable points
- Vehicle weapons are devastating to personnel

### Vehicle vs. Ship

- Vehicle weapons have minimal effect on spelljammers
- Boarding actions allow personal/vehicle scale combat inside ships

See [Vehicles](./17-Vehicles.mdx) and [Ships](./18-Ships.mdx) for scale-specific rules.

## Action Economy

The number of Free Actions per turn is **at SM discretion**. Generally allow 2–3 without question; more requires narrative justification.

| Action | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Speaking a sentence or two | Free |
| Drawing a holstered weapon | Free |
| Dropping something | Free |
| Multiple weapon swaps per turn | Probably not Free |
| Lengthy monologue | Might require a Half Action |

| 30 | Very Hard | Elite specialist at their best |
| 35 | Legendary | World-class, one-in-a-million |
| 40+ | Impossible | Superhuman or divine intervention |

## Experience & Advancement

Character growth is measured with Experience Points. There are two kinds: **banked** (available to spend) and **total** (lifetime earned, never reduced). When the SM awards XP, add it to both.

### Abstract Method (Recommended)

Award **500 XP per session**, adjusted for session length. This ensures steady, smooth power growth.

### Detailed Method

Assign XP to individual encounters based on difficulty. Award XP when an encounter is completed or overcome. Only award XP for meaningful encounters—wholesale slaughter of trivially weak enemies earns nothing.

| Difficulty  | XP Award |
| ----------- | -------- |
| Easy        | 50       |
| Routine     | 70       |
| Ordinary    | 100      |
| Average     | 130      |
| Challenging | 170      |
| Hard        | 200      |
| Very Hard   | 250      |

### Spending Experience

| Improvement              | Cost                 |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- |
| Improve a Characteristic | 100 × Current Rank   |
| Improve Devotion         | 50 × Current Rank    |
| Improve a Skill          | 50 × Current Rank    |
| Improve your Power Stat  | 200 × Current Rank\* |
| Improve a Magic School   | 100 × Current Rank\* |
| Improve a Sword School   | 100 × Current Rank\* |
| Buy a Feat               | 100                  |
| New Skill                | 100                  |
| New Magic or Sword       | 200                  |

\* Power Stat, Magic Schools, and Fighting Style ranks are all capped at your current Level.

:::tip[Clarification]
You cannot buy feats that aren't on your class lists, ever. Double XP cost for off-list feats was tried and became unbalanced very fast.
:::

### Other Rewards

Backgrounds can be earned in-game: a high-ranking position (Backing), a mystical talisman (Artifact), a lot of cash (Wealth). Fame and Status come naturally as long as activities aren't kept secret. Some Assets may also be gained through play—a Promethean rebuilt with Necrodermis, a Chosen undergoing a ceremony to gain a patron's Mark.

> **Play Tip:** Backgrounds like Wealth, Backing, and Contacts should be earned in-game by playing, not just by spending XP. These should be reasonably easy to get if the players are actively pursuing them. Each new dot represents roughly 5–10× more effect than the previous dot. **Holdings and Followers require upkeep** — characters can defer costs for a while or risk depleting their Wealth, but eventually they must cover them.
>
> **Play Tip:** At 500 XP per session, characters gain about one whole class per session—slightly less if buying magic, sword schools, and Power stat. They hit 5th level around session 6. For a longer campaign, try: 1) 100 XP for attendance, 2) 100 XP for awesome moments, 3) 100 XP for a hindrance coming into play (Enemy, Hunted, Grim Servant O' Death — SM-activated ones, not passive player-activated ones like All Thumbs, Hatred, or Illiterate).

:::tip[Optional Rule]
Devotion can be expensive — going from 8 to 9 dots is 400 XP at base price, still 200 XP for a Chosen. Alternatives: 1) All dots are 50 XP each. 2) Half price for everyone (25 XP × dots). 3) Get a free dot whenever you gain a higher level or increase your Power stat.
:::

## Common Skill Applications

### Persuasion vs. Deceive vs. Intimidation

- **Persuasion:** Honest attempts to convince through logic or appeal
- **Deceive:** Lies, half-truths, and misdirection
- **Intimidation:** Coercion through fear or threat

### Perception vs. Scrutiny

- **Perception:** Noticing physical things (hidden door, lurking assassin)
- **Scrutiny:** Reading people (detecting lies, noticing nervousness)

### Academic Lore vs. Forbidden Lore vs. Common Lore

- **Common Lore:** General knowledge everyone might know
- **Academic Lore:** Scholarly or technical knowledge
- **Forbidden Lore:** Dangerous, secret, or heretical knowledge
