> **Quick Reference:**
>
> - Alignment represents dedication to a cosmic force — choose from **The Ruinous Powers** (Chaos), **The Blessed Pantheon** (Law), or **The Gray Council** (Neutral)
> - **Devotion** (1–10) measures your commitment; higher = stricter expectations
> - Failing an **Alignment Test** (roll 1d10 >= Devotion) loses 1 Devotion and risks **Degeneration**
> - Devotion 0 = character removed from play permanently
> - Alignment is chosen during [Character Creation](./02-Char-Creation.mdx) (Step 7)

## Devotion Mechanics

### How Devotion Works

1. **Starting Devotion:** Every character begins at Devotion 6 before spending XP
2. **Alignment Test trigger:** When you violate your deity's commandments, the SM calls for a Test
3. **Roll:** Roll `1d10` — match or exceed your Devotion score (+ class feat/asset bonuses) to pass
4. **Pass:** Nothing happens
5. **Fail:** Permanently lose 1 Devotion point (can be re-purchased with XP)

### Degeneration

When a failed Alignment Test drops you to **Devotion 6 or less**, make a second check against your new score:

- **Pass:** No Degeneration
- **Fail:** Roll on the Degeneration Chart (don't lose another Devotion point)

Repeated Degeneration stacks. Reroll duplicates. If Devotion reaches **0**, the character is permanently removed from play.

### Raising Devotion

- Spend XP (maximum +1 point at a time)
- SM may require roleplay: concrete acts of contrition
- Gaining the next Devotion point cures the Degeneration assigned to that level

### Changing Alignment

| Change Type | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Same pantheon, different god | Lose 2 Devotion (minimum 1) |
| Different pantheon | Devotion set to 4; roll Degeneration at level 7; must earn new god's attention to cure |

A character may only change alignment **once**. The gods do not forgive repeated betrayal.

Roll `1d100` when suffering Degeneration:

| Roll | Effect | Mechanical Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01-07 | **Palsy** — tics and tremors | Dexterity -1 |
| 08-11 | **Dark-Hearted** — cruel and vindictive | Fellowship -1 |
| 12-21 | **Ill-Fortuned** — SM forces one reroll/session | Reroll (not Alignment Tests) |
| 22-28 | **Skin Afflictions** — boils, sores | -`2k0` to social Tests |
| 29-32 | **Morbid** — macabre visions | Intelligence -1 |
| 33-41 | **Witch-Mark** — minor physical deformity | Marks character as cursed |
| 42-45 | **Wasted Frame** — corpse-like pallor | Strength -1 |
| 46-54 | **Horrific Nightmares** — Night Terrors hindrance | Reroll if already has it |
| 55-58 | **Poor Health** — phantom pains | Constitution -1 |
| 59-62 | **Malign Sight** — world appears to rot | Wisdom -1 |
| 63-69 | **Ashen Taste** — food is disgusting | Double Fatigue level effects |
| 70-76 | **Blackouts** — unexplainable | SM determines what happens |
| 77-80 | **Distrustful** — open antipathy | Charisma -1 |
| 81-85 | **Fell Obsession** — strange fixation | Willpower -1 |
| 86-90 | **Mood Swings** — unpredictable | Composure -1 |
| 91-00 | **Blighted Mind** — mind snaps | Gain new minor derangement |

**Note:** Characteristic reductions from Degeneration cannot be improved with XP until the Degeneration is cured. Curing requires raising Devotion past that level.

## The Ruinous Powers

The gods of Chaos — anarchic, self-focused, and constantly at war with each other. Worship is seen as dangerous or criminal in most societies. They encourage followers to pursue their desires regardless of consequences.

### Khorne

**The Blood God.** God of honorable combat and slaughter. Worship through bloodshed — yours or your enemies'.

**Commandments:**

- Spill blood in his name — do not shy from violence
- Do not trust in sorcery or learn its secrets
- Fight in honorable combat, not against the weak or helpless

**Special Rule:** Khorne hates magic. If your Exalt or racial powers grant spellcasting school ranks, you may take a Sword School rank instead.

| Devotion | Sin |
| --- | --- |
| 10 | Giving mercy to a fellow warrior |
| 9 | Allowing another to kill an innocent |
| 8 | Travelling with a spellcaster |
| 7 | Showing disrespect for another warrior |
| 6 | Using stealth or trickery to avoid a fight |
| 5 | Breaking your word of honor |
| 4 | Killing an innocent |
| 3 | Allowing a spell to be cast on you |
| 2 | Refusing a challenge of combat |
| 1 | Casting a Spell |

### Slaanesh

**The God of Excess.** Perfection, novelty, hedonism, art. Worship ranges from simple pleasure-seeking to elaborate multi-day rituals.

**Commandments:**

## The Gray Council

Gods who don't align with Chaos or Law. Mostly serve their own goals. Some of the most worshipped gods — they don't urge action against other pantheons, so they're found everywhere without opposition.

### Acererak

**God of Magic and secrets.** Legend says he gave away his body piece by piece for knowledge, leaving only a skull. Knew the secret to godhood.

## Codes of Honor

_Source: Book 1 Appendix B_

Three behavioral codes providing roleplay guidance. Not mechanically enforced, but violating tenets carries narrative consequences at SM discretion.

### Principled

The strictest code:

1. Always keeps her word
2. Avoids all lies
3. Never kills or attacks unarmed foes
4. Never harms an innocent for any reason
5. Never tortures — never
6. Never kills for pleasure
7. Always tries to help others
8. Works well with others
9. Respects authority, laws, discipline, and honor
10. Never betrays a friend
11. Never breaks the law unless absolutely desperate

### Scrupulous

More flexible:

1. Keeps her word to any honorable person
2. Lies only to the dishonest and dishonorable
3. Never kills or attacks unarmed foes
4. Never harms an innocent
5. Never tortures for pleasure (may extract info from criminals)
6. Never kills for pleasure; tries to bring villains to justice alive
7. Always tries to help others
8. Works within the law whenever possible
9. Bends laws when absolutely necessary
10. Distrusts authority (may be corrupt)
11. Works with groups but dislikes confining bureaucracy

### Aberrant

Most common in practice:

1. Always keeps his word of honor
2. May lie and cheat those not worthy of respect
3. May or may not kill an unarmed foe
4. Never kills an innocent (especially children) — may kidnap
5. Never kills for pleasure
6. Never inhumane treatment of prisoners (torture distasteful but may be necessary)
7. Never tortures for pleasure
8. May or may not help someone in need
9. Works with others to attain his goals
10. Respects honor and self-discipline in others
11. Never betrays a friend
