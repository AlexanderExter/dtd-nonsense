:::note[Quick Reference]
D:TD uses ten-sided dice (`d10`). Tests roll `XkY` — roll X dice, keep the highest Y, and sum them against a Target Number (TN). Dice that roll 10 **explode** (reroll and add). Every 5 points above the TN is a **Raise**; every 5 below is a **Check**. **Hero Points** let you reroll, reduce TNs, or cheat death.
:::

---

## How to Make a Test — Step by Step

<Steps>

1. **SM calls for a Test** — identifies the Characteristic + Skill (or just Characteristic) and sets the TN.
2. **Determine your dice pool** — Rolled dice = Characteristic + Skill. Kept dice = Characteristic. Apply any bonuses or penalties.
3. **Roll and keep** — Roll all dice, keep the highest Y, discard the rest.
4. **Resolve explosions** — Any die showing 10 explodes: reroll and add. Repeat on consecutive 10s.
5. **Sum kept dice** — Total the kept dice. Apply flat modifiers (e.g., free Raises at +5 each).
6. **Compare to TN** — Meet or exceed the TN to succeed. Count Raises (each +5 over TN) or Checks (each −5 below TN).

</Steps>

## Dice Notation (`XkY`)

Roll X dice, keep the highest Y, and add them together. The shorthand is **`XkY`** — for example, `5k3` means roll 5 dice, keep the best 3.

- **Rolled dice** — the total number of dice you throw.
- **Kept dice** — the number you actually count toward your total (always the highest).

---

## Exploding Dice

When you roll a **10** on any die, it **explodes**: reroll that die and add the new result to the 10. If you roll another 10, keep going. No matter how many times a die explodes, it counts as one kept die.

Some Feats and abilities change how explosions work — these apply to both initial and follow-up explosions.

---

## Rolling More Than Ten Dice

When your rolled dice exceed 10:

- **Extra rolled dice** convert to kept dice at a **2-for-1** ratio. Example: `12k6` → drop 2 rolled, gain 1 kept → `10k7`.
- **Once you reach `10k10`**, each additional die (rolled or kept) becomes a flat **+5** bonus. Example: `15k10` → `10k10` +25. `11k11` → `10k10` +10.

---

## Target Numbers

All Tests are made against a Target Number (TN). Meet or exceed the TN to succeed.

| TN  | Difficulty             |
| --- | ---------------------- |
| 5   | Mundane                |
| 10  | Easy                   |
| 15  | Average                |
| 20  | Hard                   |
| 25  | Very Hard              |
| 30  | Heroic                 |
| 35  | Never Done Before      |
| 40  | Never to be Done Again |

The base TN for an ordinary Skill Test is **15**.

## Tests

Tests determine success or failure when actions have dramatic consequences. See also: [Characteristics & Skills](./03-Characteristics-Skills.mdx) for skill lists and governing Characteristics.

### Skill Tests

The most common Test type. Roll Characteristic + Skill dice, keep Characteristic dice, and compare to the TN.

- **Untrained Basic Skills** — roll as a Characteristic Test with **−1** to the Characteristic. Situational modifiers apply after the penalty.
- **Untrained Advanced Skills** — cannot be attempted at all.

### Characteristic Tests

When no skill applies, the SM may call for a Characteristic Test — roll and keep dice equal to the Characteristic alone.

### Rank 0 Characteristics

If a Characteristic is reduced to 0 (by penalties or curses), the character is severely impaired:

- Always roll **one die** for the Characteristic, applying Skills and specialties normally.
- If any die shows a 10, it counts as **0** instead — no explosions.

---

## Raises and Checks

After a roll, compare the total to the TN:

- **Raise** — for every **5 points** above the TN, you earn one Raise. Raises represent exceptional success.
- **Check** — for every **5 points** below the TN, you have one Check. Checks measure how badly you failed.
- **Free Raises** — if a Feat or ability grants free Raises, add **+5** per free Raise to the roll result.

Raises matter most for social skills (Charm, Persuasion), automatic fire, and any effect that scales with success. See [Combat](./14-Combat.mdx) and [Social Combat](./15-Social-Combat.mdx) for examples.

## Opposed Rolls

When two characters compete directly, both make Tests. The one with the higher total wins.

- If both succeed, highest total wins.
- If both fail, the SM may call it a stalemate or require a reroll.
- On a tie, the character with the **higher governing Characteristic** wins. If still tied, the SM decides or calls for a reroll.

**Example:** Hiding from a guard — the explorer Tests Stealth vs. the guard's Perception. Both roll; highest total wins.

## Stunting

Stunting rewards dramatic, evocative descriptions of actions with bonus **drama dice** (additional rolled dice). Stunts represent heroic characters being truly spectacular.

| Stunt Level | Bonus  | Requirements |
| ----------- | ------ | ------------ |
| **One die** | +`1k0` | Good description of the action. Enables borderline-impossible feats (running across heads in a crowd, deflecting a blade barehanded). |
| **Two dice** | +`2k0` | Interact with the environment or leverage world knowledge (enemy phobias, lover's favorite flower). Allows limited **dramatic editing** — reveal minor details without contradicting the scene (e.g., a banner on the wall to swing from). Cannot conjure weapons from nowhere. SM may veto anything that strains belief. |
| **Three dice** | +`3k0` | Singular act of greatness — must leave the table slack-jawed. If there's any doubt, it's not a three-die stunt. Includes two-die dramatic editing. |

**Guidelines:**

- Short and flowing beats long and clunky. Stringing adjectives isn't enough — the description must be interesting without interrupting play.
- Important SM characters may stunt sparingly. Unimportant characters never stunt.
- In gritty games, the SM may limit stunt effects for all characters.

## Hero Points

Hero Points represent a character's tie to fate — destiny, divine favor, or sheer luck. They separate heroes from ordinary people.

### Using Hero Points

Spending a Hero Point is a **Free Action** usable on your turn or in reaction to another's action. Each spent point reduces your pool by one. Spent points restore at the start of the next gaming session (or mid-session at SM discretion).

| Effect | Cost |
| ------ | ---- |
| **Reroll** a failed Test (final result stands) | 1 Hero Point |
| **Reduce TN** by 5 (declared before rolling) | 1 Hero Point |
| **Add a Raise** to an already-successful Test | 1 Hero Point |
| **Count as 10** for Initiative | 1 Hero Point |
| **Instantly recover** from Stunned | 1 Hero Point |

### Burning Hero Points

Burning a Hero Point **permanently** reduces your pool by one. The result: **you survive whatever would have killed you.** How you survive is negotiated with the SM — the attack wasn't fatal, you were thrown clear, etc. Consequences are optional and largely player-decided — you might walk away unscathed, or end up losing a limb or becoming heavily scarred. A Hero Point can be burnt even if it was already spent this session.

### Gaining Additional Hero Points

Awarded at SM discretion for reaching milestones, accomplishing major goals, or exceptional roleplay. Burnt points can be replenished through these awards.

:::note
Hero Points and Exaltation Resource Stats are distinct pools. Hero Points are narrative currency for all characters. Resource Stats (Mana, Rage, Essence, etc.) are exaltation-specific and refresh per the [Exaltation's](./05-Exaltations.mdx) rules.
:::
