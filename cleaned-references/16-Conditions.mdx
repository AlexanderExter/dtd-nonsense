# Conditions

Characters can suffer from a variety of conditions that affect their abilities. This section covers conditions, fear, insanity, and mental trauma.

---

## Conditions Summary

| Condition        | Effect                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Amputation       | Lose a body part. Treat as Heavily Wounded.                                    |
| Blinded          | -`4k0` to most Tests. Take Athletics + Dexterity Test (TN 15) to move Half Rate. |

## Condition Descriptions

### Amputation

When a character loses a body part the character is treated as having taken enough damage to become Heavily Wounded regardless of their current wound status. The character must use any healing that she receives on the missing limb damage only. When that damage track is recovered, the character is considered to be stabilized enough that she can go back to healing other wounds. Amputations can be replaced by cybernetics, healing magic, or regeneration, but left alone they will not grow back.

### Blinded

A character who has been inflicted with blindness suffers from a total inability to see, usually (but not always) because they no longer have any eyes. Blinded characters suffer a -`4k0` penalty to physical Tests, but do not suffer this penalty for Tests with a social element (such as Intimidation and Persuasion). A blinded character will need to pass an Athletics + Dexterity Test (TN 15) to navigate terrain more quickly than their Half Rate movement without tripping.

### Blood Loss

A character can start losing blood (or whatever they have instead of it) as a result of certain Critical Effects. Blood Loss effects are cumulative. Thus, if a character suffers a second effect that causes Blood Loss, they will be losing blood at 2 Blood Loss. At the start of every Round (starting in the Round after the character was injured), the character must roll their Constitution. If the result is less than 10 × Blood Loss, the character suffers an amount of damage equal to 1d10 per Blood Loss, regardless of Armor and Aura.

This is best treated with the First Aid skill, or with magical healing. Either will stop the Blood Loss because of a single wound or reduce Blood Loss by 1 per success from that of multiple wounds.

Lost limbs are considered to have a Blood Loss rating of 5.

### Burning

The character is on fire. She suffers 1d10 Energy damage per Round, ignoring Armor, and cannot take any actions other than trying to put herself out. This requires a successful (TN 15) Intelligence Test. Each character in melee range of the burning character can choose to help the burning character, giving them +`1k0` (but stopping themselves from doing anything productive).

### Combined Penalty

A character may suffer multiple conditions, and these conditions, even without other effects, can combine to push characters further into their wound tracks. Each wound track is a binary state, not numerical, but if a character ends up being afflicted by multiple conditions or Critical Effects that place them into Lightly Wounded, this rolls together into Heavily Wounded as they take more and more damage. Of course, this only counts for 'new' or 'current' conditions - a character with a crippled leg is already Lightly Wounded, and if she is crippled again would be Heavily Wounded, but other Conditions that place a character in Lightly Wounded will not combine with old injuries.

### Crippled

Crippling is when a limb becomes damaged and unusable. This might be because the arm is broken or a leg has been severely damaged. If an arm is crippled the character can no longer use it to attack. If a leg is crippled, the character falls prone and can only crawl along the ground at 1 meter per half action. If a vital organ is crippled, the character faces more specific effects—generally inability to perform certain actions or possibly needing technological or magical aid to survive.

### Dazzled

A Dazzled character has been dazed and confused. She takes `-1k0` to all Tests and an additional `-1k0` (that's a `-2k0` total) to all Tests based on sight.

### Deafened

A character who has been inflicted with deafness suffers a total inability to hear, usually (but not always) because they no longer have any ears. Deafened characters suffer a -`2k0` penalty to all Tests. In addition, the character will automatically fail any Tests based on hearing (at the SM's discretion).

### Fatigue

Exhaustion, combat trauma, or swapping licks with bare fists can all leave a character battered, but more or less intact. Fatigue measures the amount of non-lethal injury a character can take. Characters gain Fatigue from certain types of attacks, some critical effects, and some weapons and feats.

Fatigue is measured in levels. A character can take a number of levels of Fatigue equal to her Constitution score and still function, albeit with some side-effects. Should a character take a number of levels of Fatigue in excess of her Constitution, she collapses, unconscious, for 10 − Constitution hours. After the character awakens, her levels of Fatigue revert to a number equal to her Constitution. Characters suffering any level of Fatigue suffer `-1k0` to all Tests. A character recovers one level of Fatigue per hour as long as they can rest and relax.

### Frightened

A frightened character is too shaken to fight. For the remainder of the encounter, the character must run from the source of the fear. If the character has no way to escape the encounter space, they are unable to make attacks, use magic, or stunts. In addition, the character takes -`2k0` to all Tests while they are still frightened.

### Helpless

Anything that prevents a character from defending themselves will leave them Helpless. While Helpless, the character may not take any actions. Tests made to hit a Helpless target automatically succeed. When rolling damage against a Helpless target, roll twice and add the results.

### Pinned

A character who has been pinned is under threat and may not move from her current position or take any Actions (including reactions) other than the Full Defense action (which provides a bonus to avoid being pinned further). She can still speak, but may not take free actions other than speaking.

### Prone

A character who is knocked down is prone. Attacks against a prone character from relative melee range of the attack are made with +`2k0` bonus—far enough away and actually hitting the prone character becomes harder, and ranged attacks take -`2k0` to the attack roll. A prone character can only Move by crawling at Half Rate, and suffers -`2k0` to attack rolls. A prone character can stand up as a full round action.

### Stunned

A character that is stunned is unable to do anything except take a single move action (and go at half rate, at that). Stunned characters cannot take any Reactions.

### Toxic

A character suffering from Toxic is poisoned, diseased, or dying. A Toxic rating of 3 or greater is lethal if not treated. If the character has a Toxic rating of 1 or 2, they suffer that much damage at the start of each Round. If their Toxic rating is 3 or higher, the character takes that many d10 damage at the start of each Round. A Toughness Test (TN 15 + Toxic) at the start of each Round keeps the character from taking their Toxic damage that Round (or halves it, rounding up, if she gets at least one raise). Medical treatment, antidotes, or magical healing can remove the Toxin Rating, while the Regeneration power will remove it at the rate of 1 per Round.

## Fear and Insanity

### Fear

The Alignment Wheel has more dimensions than just two. Some characters have developed strong defense mechanisms against the Terrors of this world. Such characters have a Fear resistance of 1 or higher. For each point of Fear, a character subtracts 5 from any mental trauma damage they suffer and gains a +`2k0` bonus to any mental-related Tests to resist the terrors of the void.

Fear can be gained by increasing your Dark alignment, taking certain feats, or simply by being exposed to so many horrors that they no longer bother you. Fear can also be gained by becoming a horror yourself—some types of undead gain Fear resistance.

The terrible events of the Wheel can cause damage to a character's mental state. When faced with moments of sheer terror, a character must make a Willpower + Composure Test or suffer an amount of mental trauma damage as listed on the Shock Table. This mental trauma damage is added to the character's Trauma track.

## Mental Trauma

Mental trauma affects the character's mind. The trauma track begins at 0 and has no upper limit. Whenever a character passes certain thresholds, they suffer a Mental Disorder. These disorders are dangerous, as they can affect the character's ability to function.

### Mental Disorder Table

| Threshold | Effect                                                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 40        | The character gains 1 Minor Disorder.                   |
| 60        | The character gains 1 Severe Disorder.                  |
| 80        | The character gains 1 Acute Disorder.                   |
| 100       | The character must retire. They have become too broken. |

### Disorder Severity

**Minor Disorders** are relatively mild. They manifest primarily when the character is under stress. A Minor Disorder might cause a character to take -`1k0` to social Tests with certain kinds of people or become briefly catatonic in certain situations.

**Severe Disorders** are serious conditions that significantly impact one's life. A Severe Disorder might cause a character to become obsessive, develop a phobia that leaves them unable to act, or develop violent personality shifts.

**Acute Disorders** are debilitating. A character with an Acute Disorder has great difficulty functioning in society. They might become completely unhinged, develop dangerous compulsions, or lose touch with reality entirely.

## Therapy

_Source: Book 1 Appendix C_

A character wishing to reduce their Mental Trauma score through therapy follows these steps:

**Step 1 — Treatment Period:** The character undergoes therapy sessions (twice per week minimum) for 2d10 − Willpower months (minimum 2 months). Under constant institutional care with professional therapists and medication, reduce months to weeks.

**Step 2 — Therapist Assessment:** The therapist Tests Academic Lore + Intelligence (or Scrutiny + Wisdom) against TN 80 minus the patient's current Trauma score. On success, the therapist then Tests Persuasion + Fellowship at the patient's Trauma score as TN, gaining +5 per Raise on the first Test. Up to two assistants may aid the therapist.

**Step 3 — Patient Breakthrough:** If Step 2 succeeds and the patient has not gained more than 10 Trauma since the last successful attempt, the patient Tests Wisdom + Composure − Forbidden Lore dots against their Trauma score as TN. The patient adds +1 per Devotion dot and +5 per Raise from Step 2. Only a Spirit Mentor may aid this Test.

**Step 4 — Recovery:** On success, reduce Trauma by 1d5 + 2 per Raise, capped at 1/10 of the patient's current Trauma score.

> **Critical Failure:** If all dice on any of these Tests come up as 1s, the patient gains 1 point of Trauma.

## Healing Mental Trauma

Mental trauma heals more slowly than physical wounds. Beyond professional therapy (above), a character can reduce their Trauma through:

- **Rest and Recuperation:** Extended downtime in safe environments.
- **Magical Healing:** Some spells and abilities can heal mental wounds.
- **Fulfilling Alignment:** Acting strongly in accordance with one's Alignment can restore mental stability.

The SM should work with the player to determine appropriate recovery methods and timeframes for their character's specific traumas and disorders.
