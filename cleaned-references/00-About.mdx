## About This Project

This is a fan-made reference site for _Dungeons the Dragoning 40,000: 7th Edition_ — a tabletop RPG blending Warhammer 40K aesthetics with D&D and World of Darkness mechanics.

The original source books are dense, hard to search, and harder to reference at the table. This project transforms them into a searchable, cleaned, cross-referenced documentation site with interactive play tools.

## What's Different Here

- Full text of both source books — cleaned, and cross-referenced
- Topic-based reference pages (not chapter-by-chapter) for quick lookup
- 5 interactive play tools: Character Builder, Character Sheet, Combat Tracker, NPC Generator, Ship Builder
- Quick Reference page integrated into the rules documentation
- Searchable via full-text search (Pagefind)

## FAQ

Q : Who the hell are you?

A: I'm just a random guy that was around when the books hit /tg and loved this moment of brilliant madness since.

Q: This is vibe coded.

A: That is not a question. But yes, it is.

Q: Why did you bring slop to our temple of homebrew?!

A: Multiple Reasons:
1) As part of my work I was asked to test the utility and effect of a corporate level agent tool. This meant I needed to run a meaningful project of some scale. This felt like a nice goal that would answer my questions and leave me with something useful when done.
2) My playground plays this game frequently, so we have a personal reason to have a site such as this. I may as well make something for the community.
3) I used this as a learning experience and I personally enjoy figuring things out.


Q: You butechered the books, the holy texts of LawfulNice! You think you are better than him?

A: No, I kinda fucked up early in the project. My initial goal was to port the PDFs to Markdown, then correct typos and organize what was there, verbatim. But my covnersion process was very poor and many lessons were learned. What I ended up with is an edited version of the books that is most certainly not the original ones. Its mostly accurate and debatably more readable. In the future, I want to obtain a higher quality Raw markdown to do that properly.

I'd be happy to recieve some help on this particular point.

Q: Why are there no dice rollers but dice pool references?

A: Design decision, I specifically do not want this tool to co opt table play and the face to face aspect. So the experiece of dice rolling is intentionally left out. For the nerds, getting True Random on a computer is nontrivial. There are plenty good solutions for all use cases out there, including you using actual fucking dice.

Q: Why is there no log in or character or camapaing save features?

A: Two reasons:

1) As a random internet guy, I have no fucking business asking for your identity or personal data even if its only an email.
2) Having that kind of features requires that I have a database and a front-end/back-end architecture. This is more complex, difficult to secure and incurs ongoing operating costs. One of my goals here is for this to last even if I can't mantain it. In the future I might consider expanding this, or accepting help. But currently not the plans.

Q: Why didn't you add The Big Book of Brew or (insert x homebrew found on the forums)

A: Getting the core books into a coherent state and referenced was difficult enough and I could work them as a static goal. I did link the forums and nothing prevents you form using said content. But for start I wanted to get the core elements working. Also, every homebrew is a matter of opinion and I don't want to force every group to play with every rule a nerd ever added.
