# Advanced Rules

This chapter contains optional and advanced rules for specialized situations: daemonic possession, tracking, astropaths and navigators, zero-gravity combat, explosives, orbital strikes, animal companions, and the Umbra.

---

## Daemonic Possession

### Standard Daemon Possession

If using standard Daemons, apply to the victim:

1. Increase Strength, Constitution, and Willpower by 1 each (Lesser Daemon) or 2 each (Greater Daemon). Can exceed 5.
2. Add traits: Daemonic (+Con to AP & HP), DarkSight, Resource
3. Increase victim's Level by 1 (max: daemon's level)
4. Increase Resilience by 1
5. Recalculate Hit Points, Speed, etc.
6. Greater Daemons also get warp fire breath weapon

### Modified/Random Daemon Possession

1. Increase Strength, Constitution, and Resilience by 1. Level +1 if less than daemon's Level.
2. Add DarkSight trait; make them a Daemonhost exalt with Power stat equal to daemon's Level (magic school, bonus armor, Resource Points)
3. Add daemon's feats, special powers, magic schools, gun katas, or sword schools
4. Use better of daemon's or victim's skills
5. Recalculate Hit Points, Speed, etc.
6. Greater Daemons also get warp fire breath weapon

### Example Combination

**Lesser Daemon Stats:** Str/Dex/Con: 3, Int: 2, Wis/Will: 3, Cha: 5, Fel: 1, Cmp: 2, Static Defense: 20, Armor(all): 10, Resilience: 4, HP: 9, Init: +7, Speed: 6, Level: 2, Size: 4, Social Defense: 15, Resource Points: 12. Skills: Weaponry 2, Ballistics 2, Brawl 3, others. Feats: Cleave, Iron Jaw, Devastating Critical, Furious Assault. Abilities: Fear Caster, Illusion spells.

**Soldier Stats:** Physicals 3, else 2. Ballistics 2, Weaponry 2. Flak Armor (5 AP arms/body, 4 AP head). Lasgun, knife. Level 2.

**Combined Possessed Soldier:** Str 4, Dex 3, Con 4, Level 2, Resource 8. Speed 7, Size/Resilience 4/5, Static Defense 17, HP 8. AP: Flak + daemon +6(all, not vs magic/silver). Illusion `4k2`, spells. Fearless(Angry). Enhanced melee attacks.

## Tracking

### Following Tracks

Default TN: 15. Apply the following modifiers as guidelines.

**Numbers:**

- Subtract the number of creatures after the first from TN. Thirty elephants are easy to track.
- Subtract twice the Size of the largest creature if they must squeeze through spaces.

**Terrain:**

| Modifier | Terrain Type                                             |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| -10      | Easily retains signs (sandy beach, light snow)           |
| +10      | Doesn't retain signs (building interiors, running water) |

**Entity:**

| Modifier | Entity Type                                                      |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| -20      | Obvious marks (giant slug slime trails)                          |
| -10      | Bleeding/leaking things                                          |
| +10      | Minimal marks (small snake in grass, person not touching things) |
| +20      | Phasing creatures leaving minimal ectoplasm                      |

**Senses:**

| Modifier | Sense Situation                                                                           |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| -10      | Tracking with a sense the creature doesn't know about (escapees unaware of tracking dogs) |
| +10      | Creature knows the tracking sense and can counter it                                      |
| +20      | Using wrong sense for the job (smell in perfume shop)                                     |

**Time:**

| Modifier | Time Factor                                               |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| -20      | Creature just moved out of sight/hearing with fresh spoor |
| -10      | Nothing has obscured signs                                |
| +10      | Something has covered/removed some signs                  |
| +20      | Very few signs left (couple days old, beach after tide)   |

**Identification:**

| Modifier | Identification Difficulty                            |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| -20      | Easily identifiable marks (rabbit vs. cougar tracks) |
| -10      | Distinguishing features provide decent guess         |
| +10      | Little information, maybe a clue or two              |
| +20      | Difficult signs or no specific markings              |

## Astropaths & Navigators

### The Setting

The setting is intentionally incomplete and contradictory, giving SMs freedom to adapt. Here are considerations for Astropaths and Navigators.

### Astropaths

Consider: speed and reliability, what they can communicate, how many exist, who controls them.

**If messages match spelljammer speed:** Astropaths reserved for off-route communications and secure faction networks. Messages sent multiple times to ensure accuracy.

**If faster than spelljammers:** Massive demand for services.

**Broadcast vs. Connection Models:**

- **Broadcast:** General cries for help, messages without knowing if reception is possible, interception by other Astropaths
- **Connection (Familiarity):** Like cellphones—Alice messages Bob directly
- **Connection (Location):** Like landlines—call a location, whoever's there receives it

**Communication Options:**

1. **No Astropaths:** Communication at spelljammer speed
2. **Astropaths only talk:** Summaries spread quickly, details are slow (Star Wars model)
3. **Astropaths send data:** May lead to telecom faction control (BattleTech model)

**Recommended Settings:**

1. No Astropaths—communication by spelljammer
2. Relatively rare (specialist doctor level), need special equipment, contact by meeting in Warp "locations," can only talk (no data transfer)

### Navigators

Every spelljammer requires a Navigator with Divination magic school dots. This implies either few spelljammers or many sorcerers with 1-2 Divination dots.

Possibilities: Navigators Guild controlling trade, national training camps, schools/unions. Remember: many people can cast Augury or Detect Thoughts.

## Zero-G Combat

### Key Concept: Braced

Being **Braced** means secured against something stable: holding a strut, tied to equipment, hooked around a pipe, glued to wall, clipped to tie-down, or using magnetic boots on ship hulls. Taking a Half Action to Brace counts for heavy weapons.

**Out of Control condition:** Half normal rolled dice for everything. Stop by grabbing something and bracing.

**Recovery tests (TN 15, free raise with appropriate equipment):** Athletics, Acrobatics, Brawling, Tech-Use, Weaponry (whips), Ballistics (grappling hooks), Piloting (jetpacks).

Braced prevents Out of Control; not being Braced doesn't cause it.

### Weapon Ranges

- Thrown/muscle-powered projectiles: ranges tripled
- Other weapons: unaffected

### Melee Damage

- Effective Strength halved unless Braced
- Unaffected: Grappling damage, parrying weapons (short stabs), Power Field weapons

### Summary

If not Braced: cumulative -`1k0` until you stop to brace. Movement usually TN 15, stopping TN 10. Explosions: test against damage or get knocked off/Out of Control.

### Combat Penalties

**Melee:** Cumulative -`1k0` per attack unless Braced. Carries over between turns.

**Ranged:** Same penalty per shot fired. Full Auto Burst without Bracing: lose +`2k1` bonus; apply penalty equal to half RoF. Half Action to Brace resets penalties to zero.

### Movement

Without flight: climb, jump (10× distance per round in straight line, free raise), or artificial propulsion (jetpacks, projectile recoil).

**Useless for propulsion:** Directed energy weapons, needlers, rocket launchers

**Good for propulsion:** Flamers (lower but steadier thrust)

**Direction changes:** Dexterity (+ Acrobatics), TN 15 modified by speed/precision. Near stop = TN 10.

### Explosions

**If Braced:** Athletics + Strength vs damage to stay put

**If not Braced:** Acrobatics + Dexterity vs damage to avoid Out of Control (still pushed away)

### Zero-G Creatures

Natural zero-G creatures: can fly or have bracing method, automatically get two free raises on rolls.

Immaterial/Phasing creatures: don't roll, just move.

## Explosives

### The Blast vs. Bag of Rats Problem

When you miss with a blast weapon, where does it go? What's the TN to land a grenade in the middle of a group?

### Optional Blast Rules

**Missing:** Miss by 1m per range increment (1m short, 2m normal, 3m long, 4m extreme) plus 1m per Check. Randomize scatter direction. Consider 3D perspective for arcing shots.

**Grouping:** Roll attack, add twice blast radius to hit things, subtract blast radius to miss things. Try to place blast matching hit/miss results.

**Can't target people further apart than blast diameter.**

**Process:**

1. Roll attack, add 2× blast to total
2. Check against all Static Defenses
3. Find placement getting everyone hit and excluding everyone missed
4. If impossible, maximize intended targets; shooter chooses among equal options
5. On miss, minimum scatter distance = enough to miss whole group

**Dodging:** Not direct attack—can't be dodged without two-die stunt using environment for cover (still just cover AP).

**Edge Cases:**

- Someone in middle hit, no one else: place as miss on everyone; random shrapnel hit the target
- Someone in middle missed: place blast catching everyone, something blocked them

### Missing Allies

When trying to hit enemies near allies:

**Roll attack, calculate both:**

- Attack + 2× blast (to hit enemies)
- Attack - 2× blast (to miss ally—ally's Static Defense is TN)

**Results:**

| Attack - 2× blast vs Ally | Attack + 2× blast vs Enemies | Result                                                              |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Under all                 | Under all                    | Miss All (scatter away)                                             |
| Over Ally's defense       | Over Enemies' defenses       | Miss Ally & Hit Enemies (ideal placement)                           |
| Under Ally's defense      | Over Enemies' defense        | Hit All (maximize hits; ally may be excluded if necessary to do so) |

## Animal Companions

The feats Divine Bond, Obtain Familiar, and Animal Companion allow loyal companion creatures.

### Animal Companion Options

**Minion Rules:** Begins as one creature with Threat and Damage ratings of 1.

**Animalistic Creature:** Befriended creatures don't take well to bionics or combat drugs. Mistreatment counts as Druid's Oath violation ("respecting nature" clause).

### Improved Animal Companion

**Minion Version:** Number of minions = character Level (if not already higher). Each time gained: +1 to Threat or Damage rating (max 5 each).

**Creature Version:** Level = higher of normal Level or (character Level - 1). Each time gained: choose one feat from Bonded Companion Feat List, increase one physical or mental attribute (except Intelligence) by 1. Can't increase same attribute twice in a row.

**Bonded Companion Feat List:** Armor Proficiency (Light, Medium, or Heavy), Blind Fighting, Cat-Fall, Evasion, Defensive Mobility, Heightened Senses (Any), Light Sleeper, Danger Sense, Sound Constitution, Weapon Focus (Any), Combat Mastery

### Beastmaster

**Minion Version:** +1 Threat and +1 Damage (max 5 each), squad size = character Level + 1.

**Creature Version:** +1 to one physical characteristic, +1 to one mental characteristic, +1 Size, +1 Level. Assign one feat and one attribute:

**Feats:** Devastating Critical, Fearless (Angry), Improved Weapon Focus, Iron Jaw, Swift Attack, True Grit

**Attributes:** Armor(4), Aura(4), DarkSight, Regeneration(1), Unnatural Toughness

### Familiars

Choose from: industrial servitor (no arc welder), animal/creature, zombie, spirit/ghost, or elemental. Reduce Size and Strength to 1-2, Level to character's Level (minimum 1).

Each character level increase: familiar gains 1 dot in any attribute or skill (can't increase same one twice in a row).

Replacement familiars don't have increased attributes/skills. Familiars can use appropriate weapons/armor and may be given bionics (if fleshy or constructed).

## The Umbra

### Overview

The Umbra is the space between the material world and the Warp. Entry methods: powerful werewolf mirror-stepping, wraith shifting, ghost/daemon sliding, sorcerer portals/rituals.

The barrier ("Veil") thickness matches how close the Warp is to the material world and how easy magic is to use.

### Perception

Experiences vary: slightly out-of-focus photographic negative, washed out and gray, sepia-toned, thick fog obscuring material world while Umbra is clear, dim shadows with lurking horrors. People together may disagree on visuals but agree on what exists there.

### Topography

Roughly parallels material world. Only permanent structures (ground, large plants, permanent buildings) are solid. Non-permanent things (people, doors, shrubs, water) are translucent or invisible.

**Warning:** Don't exit Umbra at ocean bottom unprepared.

Places of natural forces (volcanoes, vortexes, waterfalls) feel nearly normal with extra-large elementals.

### Magic in the Umbra

Magic is visible as neon geometric forms, twisting ribbons, sparkles. It's tangible—wards function as doors and walls.

**Breaking Wards:** Barrier rating = Focus Power test. Damage enough to reduce below minimum success threshold.

**Constraints:** Ranged attacks pass through without effect. Must use bare hands, artifact melee weapons, or spells.

**Destructive Failure:** When spell/ward breaks, it explodes. Radius = 1/5 original Focus Power in meters. Damage = remaining Focus Power result (magic damage, requires Aura).

### Deeper Umbra

Additional dimension toward the Warp. Moving deeper requires 1d5+5 Level/Power stat tests (TN 10 + 2 per step from material).

- Material world fades after third Test
- Landscape shifts faster and more severely near Warp
- Two checks from Warp: Psychic Phenomena roll every few rounds
- One check from Warp: Perils of the Warp Test every 1d5 rounds
- Next Test: actual Warp (bad for most people)

Return requires same Tests in reverse.

### Other Destinations

Accessible from Umbra: Webways, Commoragh, Sigil, Abyss realms (including Ruinous Powers), possibly Celestia.

**Finding locations:** TN 20 Forbidden Lore for known places, TN 25-30 for Webways and similar. Raises reduce travel time/difficulty; failure adds +1d5 to TN; failure with checks means wrong destination, Warp storm, or hostile encounter. Connection to destination = free raise.

### Building Umbral Realms

Requires reaching middle area and performing difficult rituals (TN 35+) over time. Participants get free raise finding that place.

Bringing building materials attracts wandering creatures.

**Technology:** Electronics and simple chemical reactions work. Internal combustion, rockets, anti-grav, nuclear power don't work (except small/simple, Syrnth magitech, and Super Solenoid Engines).

### Umbral Creatures

Spirits, elementals, ghosts, wraiths, daemons, powerful spellcasters and werewolves, occasional dragons or aboleths.

**No Modrons:** Their existence is antithetical. Modron areas look monochrome, geometric, movement becomes impossible.

**Native creature traits:** DarkSight plus: Constitution + Willpower armor/aura, toxic/tearing attacks, pseudo-spells (using Level + Wisdom), Amorphous, Stuff of Nightmares, Regeneration, Phasing. Immune to material world drugs/toxins except Null and Spook.
